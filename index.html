What the program that decides can and cannot do PROVEN · mentl why

CAN

  • ✓ act on each reading it is given
  • ✓ write a command for Spin and for Pulse
  • ✓ keep its own state of charge and timers between readings

CANNOT

  • ⊘ reach the network while deciding
  • ⊘ touch the filesystem while deciding
  • ⊘ make any call outside the program while deciding: no socket, no file, no clock
  • ⊘ allocate memory while deciding
  • ⊘ stop with an error while deciding
  • ⊘ ask Spin for more than 100 kW, or Pulse for more than 300 kW
  • ⊘ accept a frequency outside 55 to 65 Hz as real

✓ Proven

  
This software can't reach the internet while it decides.

  
Every decision you just watched was made by software written in Mentl, a language I built. Its compiler checks every function the decision calls, and proves the decision can't reach the internet, a file or the clock, and never allocates memory. It acts only on the numbers it is handed. Code added later that tries to reach the internet won't build, as long as the rule stays on the function.

  
Not proven: that its decisions are good. They are tested on recordings.

  
Checked by Mentl's compiler: mentl check examples/grid/station.mn

  






  
Tomorrow, hour by hour

  
Through rush hour the battery carries the building. In cleaner hours it recharges.

  

  
PacifiCorp East is the balancing area that covers most of Utah. EIA's hourly data puts tomorrow's highest demand from noon to 4 PM. In those hours the model's battery carries the building's whole 300 kW. Outside them, in any hour when at least 40 percent of the power is clean, it recharges. These are the controller's own rules.

  
Demand: EIA-930 hourly data for PacifiCorp East; tomorrow is the mean of the last seven days. Clean share: EIA-930 generation by fuel (nuclear, hydro, solar, wind and geothermal). The Station is a model, simulated.






  
✕ Refused

  
One line broke the rule.

  let price = price_m("https://api.eia.gov/…")
  
Say an AI assistant adds this line to the split-second decision, to check electricity prices online. The hour's plan comes from EIA's data, read before the decision runs. The decision itself is marked as never reaching the internet, so the compiler won't build the program. Its error names the path:

  
decide calls price_m → price_m calls fetch_param → fetch_param reaches the Network

  
The compiler's own output: mentl check examples/grid/station-leak.mn
