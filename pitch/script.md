# The two pitches

The slot is 8 to 25 minutes including questions, set by how many teams took each bounty (6 teams: 8 min, 4: 12, 3: 16, 2: 25). Each pitch below is a five-minute core; the beats marked *optional* go in when the slot allows. Ask Tyler for the team counts on Wednesday morning and pick the options before the first rehearsal of the day.

**Before the first word, both rooms.** The page live and kiosk, the number moving. The guitar on its stand, cable in, gain at the rehearsed mark, never touched live. Bitwig on workspace 1 with the tuner on the live input and `samples/event_gb.wav` on a second track, cued. Workspace 3 a terminal in the Mentl checkout. The small speaker at a quarter. Hands off the keyboard until the first beat.

**The rule of names.** The Torus room hears "the program that decides" until the certificate; "Mentl" is said once there. The Nucleus room hears "Mentl" in the first sentence. No identifier is ever spoken: not decide, not price_m, not fetch_param; the screen shows them and the mouth says "the path".

**One idea per beat.** Each cell below carries one point. If a sentence is lost, the point is what stays.

## Jargon, translated on every main screen

ROCOF is "falling at"; the trigger, 0.1 Hz per second, is "pitch falling faster than three cents a second". THD is "how clean the wave is". The deadband is "the band where nothing needs to happen", one cent wide. Droop is "the harder it falls, the harder Spin pushes", a compressor. Spin's rating is "its hundred kilowatts", never "ten C". `!Network` is "cannot reach the network". A refinement is "a number that cannot leave its range". One cent at 60 Hz is 35 millihertz. The clean share is said in percent. NDJSON is never said.

## Questions, and the answers

- **Where does the Station stay out of traffic? Show me the ledger moving.** `H`: the clock set to tomorrow's noon, RUSH HOUR on the Station, Pulse at three hundred kilowatts, the ledger counting what stays off the grid. On the hosted page it is the first minute of the recording.
- **Why a new language instead of Rust?** Rust prevents memory bugs, not effects. A Rust controller can call the network from anywhere and no compiler will say so.
- **Why not Simulink or SCADE?** They verify models and generate C. They cannot state "never reaches the network" as an obligation through every callee, cannot prove a function allocation-free, cannot derive a learning rule from the chain it learns on, and are neither open nor web-native.
- **What is the trusted base?** The compiler is self-hosting and its pinned binary reproduces itself byte for byte. The verifier is sound for what it decides and incomplete by design: effects and types are judged by `check`; a bound it cannot decide is listed as pending by `audit` and printed on every `run`, never passed as proven. One false proof is known and documented, deep recursion against a no-trap claim.
- **Why negate Network, an effect your own library declared? Why not the host boundary?** Both. The row negates WASI too, so no host call of any kind, socket, file or clock, can happen inside a decision; Network and Filesystem are named beside it because they are the words a reviewer asks about.
- **The row carries Trap. What traps?** A feedback line's store and an integer division can trap in principle, and the medium says so instead of passing a no-trap claim it has not proven. If the controller process dies, the director restarts it and the model's charge starts from its initial value; a real Station's charge is a measurement, not this program's memory.
- **Is the proof real or a comment?** `mentl check examples/grid/station-leak.mn` in the terminal: the refusal with the chain. `mentl why examples/grid/station.mn decide WASI`: "the row of decide carries no WASI". `A` on the certificate shows the audit's own text.
- **How does WebAssembly run on a controller?** The proof is at compile time; the artifact is one WASI module; it runs wherever wasmtime runs, which includes the Linux supervisors beside inverters. It answers each reading in under a millisecond here. A native backend is on the roadmap; the pedal runs the ear natively today.
- **Isn't this what Lasso does?** Lasso dispatches, learns and secures the perimeter. This proves the program beneath it and explains each decision. The layer under Lasso, not a replacement.
- **Doesn't your inverter already hear the grid?** Yes, every cycle, and better than a guitar. The guitar is how a room hears it. The new things are the proof, the explanation, and the fleet seeing angle.
- **Why zero crossings and not a PLL?** Three estimators run side by side and agree within three millihertz; crossings are the simplest to read. The learner is the PLL's cousin, written as one learned coefficient, its gradient derived by the compiler.
- **Is a guitar accurate?** A few millihertz, calibrated against the grid's long-run mean of 60.000; the offset is printed on the screen.
- **A level drop is not anti-islanding.** Correct, and the screen says "the hum died". Real anti-islanding is frequency and voltage windows under IEEE 1547, which the same measurement feeds.
- **Is the station real?** Modeled from Torus's published figures, labeled on every screen, as the brief allows.
- **Why is the rush hour at noon?** Because PacifiCorp East's data says so this week: the valley's commercial and industrial load peaks midday in October; the evening is when the sun leaves. Both are on the page.
- **Is the forecast real?** EIA files a day-ahead demand forecast for PacifiCorp East; the panel draws it when it is filed and the seven-day shape until then, and its caption says which.
- **Where is price?** In the hour's plan, where the Station reads it before the reflex. A price read inside the reflex is the line the compiler refuses.
- **What's the business?** Paid pilots with asset operators, first and only this year. The proof gate for teams running coding agents is the same compiler sold to the pilot's engineering team, second. The SBIR lane is non-dilutive runway for the assurance tool, not a market.
- **Which solicitation, when, and who signs the end-user memo?** AFWERX's SBIR open topic, which opens several times a year; the window after day 60. The end-user memo is what the day-60 conversation with the 309th is for: their need, in their words. Registration (SAM.gov, SBIR.gov, DSIP) is free and takes weeks, so it is day one, not day 30.
